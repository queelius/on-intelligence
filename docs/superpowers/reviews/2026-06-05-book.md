### SUBSTANTIVE

#### [S1] Ch16 recursive-self-improvement statistic rests on a single, soft-cited source and is stated with surprising precision

- **Location:** `chapters/16_whats_ahead.tex:144` (and the supporting Further Reading entry `chapters/98_reading.tex:59`)
- **Finding:** The passage reports that "the company that builds one of the frontier models reported that its own model wrote more than four-fifths of the code merged into its production systems, up from almost none a year and a half earlier, and that its engineers were merging several times the code per day they had managed two years before." This is the single most specific, most contestable empirical claim in the new material, and it is load-bearing for the "the trajectory has a mechanism that can steepen it" argument. Its only anchor is a self-reported, non-peer-reviewed company blog post ("When AI builds itself," `anthropic.com`, 2026). The "more than four-fifths" and "several times the code per day" figures are precise enough that a skeptical reader will want the number pinned, and a single vendor's self-report about its own productivity is exactly the kind of claim a careful book should hedge harder. Auditors: math/fact + quality (both independently flagged the sourcing).
- **Suggested fix:** Soften the precision and mark the provenance in-text: e.g., "one frontier lab reported (about its own systems, so read with that in mind) that its model wrote the majority of the code merged into production." Either keep the four-fifths figure with an explicit "by the company's own account" qualifier, or round to "the majority." The Further Reading entry already flags the caveat; mirror that caution in the body.

- **Location:** `chapters/14_reward_and_reasoning.tex:8-9`

Every other chapter epigraph cites a locatable work (book + year, or paper + year). For a book whose stated rule is that every claim be traceable, this epigraph is the one that is not. Auditors: quality + spec.
- **Suggested fix:** Give it a locatable home: Sutton's statement is canonically associated with his course materials and is quoted in Sutton & Barto (2nd ed., 2018), which is already in Further Reading. Attribute as "Richard S. Sutton, the reward hypothesis (as stated in Sutton & Barto, 2018)" or cite the original lecture-note source.
