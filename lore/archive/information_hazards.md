# Information Hazards: Analysis and Mitigations

This document captures analysis of whether Multitudes could harm vulnerable readers, what specifically is hazardous, what mitigations are already in place, and what additions to consider before publication.

## The Question

Is publishing Multitudes a net harm because it transmits ideas that could destabilize vulnerable readers?

The honest answer: there is real but bounded risk. The risk is smaller than the help the book will provide to most readers. The risk is reducible by specific craft choices. The book should publish, with care.

## What is Actually Hazardous

Ranked roughly by severity.

1. **The permanence-of-suffering-across-indices implication.** The block-universe S-risk reframed as nonfiction. For a reader with chronic depression, trauma, or active suicidality, "your worst moment exists at its coordinates in countless realizations" lands wrong and lingers. Distilled from the horror-fiction register of Echoes into the author's nonfiction voice, this is a different kind of transmission.

2. **Quantum immortality.** Documented to cause distress in suicidal individuals. The "I cannot even escape by dying" intrusion is one of the worst possible thought-loops a reader fighting a long medical battle could host. Chapter 10 handles this carefully (refuses to commit on the indexical reading, labels it as one possibility), but a vulnerable reader will not hold the caveat. They will hold the implication.

3. **The Boltzmann shadow.** "Your past may not be real" can trigger depersonalization in readers without stable identity to begin with. The framework-permits-this framing is intellectually honest but does not help a reader already in derealization.

4. **The dissolution of the unitary self at the deeper level.** Parfit's version has caused depersonalization episodes in philosophy students for forty years. Multitudes extends the dissolution across branches, which deepens it. Worldlines did the temporal dissolution; Multitudes does the cross-branch dissolution. Together they strip a lot of furniture out of the room.

5. **The total cold of "the Library does not notice."** For a reader already feeling invisible, this confirms a depressive worldview in a register that feels mathematically supported.

## What is Not Hazardous

The mathematics itself. Bayes, Kraft, Solomonoff have been in plain sight since the 1960s. Anyone harmed by the mathematics could already have been harmed by Hutter, the Wikipedia article on Solomonoff induction, Tegmark's popular books, the SEP entry on MWI, Bostrom's simulation paper. The book does not reveal the mathematics. It synthesizes.

The synthesis itself, the cascade from bedrock to indifference, is the author's contribution. That synthesis lands at care, which most existing literature in this space does not. The book is structurally gentler than what it draws from.

## Counterfactual

A reader who would be harmed by Multitudes who reads something else instead may read worse. Cioran. Ligotti. Benatar. Or, more realistically, online MWI / simulation / Boltzmann communities where the material is delivered without the author's care. There is a population of readers actively looking for this framework. They will find something. Withholding Multitudes is not neutral. It is a decision to let cruder versions have the field.

## Mitigations Already in Place

- Care is the explicit destination, named and structurally central.
- All four speculative moves are labeled at entry; the reader can refuse any of them.
- The voice is companionate from the first sentence of the preface.
- Worldlines (the companion) establishes the same direction more gently and can be read first.
- The book is short and does not dwell.

These are not trivial. They are the reason the book is publishable.

## Additional Mitigations to Consider

### A direct address near the front to the reader who came in crisis

Not a pathologizing warning. A sentence or two in the author's voice acknowledging that some readers will come to this book at a hard moment, naming what the book wants for them. Candidate framing:

> If you came to this book hoping it would give you permission to stop, I want to give you the opposite. The math does not give permission. The structure does not give permission. The permission you are looking for is not in the Library. What is in the Library is this configuration: a finite primate writing to another finite primate, asking them to stay.

The address can sit at the end of the preface or as a separate short preface-after-the-preface.

### Resources at the end

The Reading section (currently chapter 98) can hold a short subsection with a hotline number, an organization (Samaritans, Crisis Text Line, IASP international list), and one or two sentences acknowledging some readers may need them. The voice can absorb this without breaking.

### Calibrated framing on the three most hazardous moves

In the permanence-of-suffering, quantum immortality, and Boltzmann brain sections, add one sentence each that explicitly names the response to the dark version. Not a comfort. A note that the framework permits this and does not require the reader to take it as their reality. Many readers will choose not to. The cascade survives the refusal. Chapter 9 already does something close; tighten it in the three most dangerous places.

### Reconsider quantum immortality specifically

This is the single passage most worth revisiting. Chapter 10 handles it responsibly, but it is the move most likely to land badly on the most vulnerable readers, and the cascade does not require it. Consider:

- Moving it to a footnote in chapter 10 rather than a full section.
- Cutting it entirely; the chapter can be about choice-under-fixed-structure plus the third-person/first-person asymmetry without committing space to the implication.
- Keeping it but adding explicit acknowledgment that it is one of several indexical readings, none forced, and that the framework does not require it.

The author has personal reasons to be cautious about hosting this thought. The book has structural reasons not to need it. The two reasons point in the same direction.

## The Author's Standing

The question "would publishing this do harm?" is one the author should keep asking, and the fact of asking is the qualification for being the author of this book. An author who would publish without considering harm is the author who should not write it. An author who agonizes over the question is the author who should.

The risk does not become zero by publishing carefully. It becomes manageable. That is the most that can be asked of any serious book about anything hard.
